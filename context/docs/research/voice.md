## 9. Tone notes

All INFERRED from the quotes above.

- INFERRED: Two voices sit side by side. The older product pages are benefit-led and somewhat generic ("Win Across The Board", "Unmatched Usability"). The newer copy (/homepage/, /why-hypergate/, 2026 blog) is sharper and built around one tension: usability vs control, cloud vs on-prem, with "data sovereignty" as the frame.
- INFERRED: The 2026 blog is technical and practical, written for IT admins and mobile engineers. It names Microsoft deadlines (NTLM, RC4, Entra Kerberos), competitor gaps (Knox, Acronis Cyber Files end of life) and gives code. This is the most specific voice they have.
- INFERRED: Core promise repeated everywhere is "no new servers, no middleware, works with what you have". Second promise is "the phone works like your Windows PC".
- INFERRED: Swiss, security-first, conservative buyer focus (government, banks, insurers, law firms, police).
- INFERRED: Copy has visible polish issues: typos ("SMB/CIFS Suport", "ect.", "by themself", "turns your mobile device in a fully fledged"), mixed British and American spelling ("enrolment" vs "enrollment", "Maximise", "centre"), and inconsistent product naming ("Single Sign On" vs "Single Sign-On", "Filemanager"). Relevant if we write copy in their voice.
- INFERRED: Uses em dashes and en dashes in site copy. Our house rule bans em dashes, so we should not copy that habit.
- INFERRED: Leans on "seamless" heavily; a fresher word may help differentiate in our copy.
